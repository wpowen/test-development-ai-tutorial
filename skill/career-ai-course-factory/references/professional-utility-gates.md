# Professional Utility Gates

## Reject immediately

Reject a course when any statement is true:

- removing AI leaves essentially the same course;
- the learner receives only prompts, slides, or plausible prose;
- no result can turn red on a meaningful failure;
- the workflow has no profession-specific artifact, metric, or decision;
- the demo hides credentials, source data, manual repair, or unavailable infrastructure;
- tool marketing substitutes for execution evidence;
- the lesson teaches button locations without a durable concept or fallback.

## Eight-gate review

Score 0-2 per gate. Require 13/16 and no zero on gates 1-5.

1. **AI centrality**: AI is the engine, system under test, agent, judge, or infrastructure.
2. **Professional authenticity**: input, output, constraints, and failure costs match real work.
3. **Runnable proof**: learner can execute or directly inspect a deterministic path.
4. **Test sensitivity**: a relevant regression is injected and detected.
5. **Reusable artifact**: dataset, Skill, code, config, checklist, report, or CI gate survives the video.
6. **Evidence quality**: capability and demand claims are source-backed and current.
7. **Beginner transfer**: prerequisites, commands, expected output, recovery, and boundaries are explicit.
8. **Maintenance**: tool/model versions, adapters, fallbacks, and refresh triggers are recorded.

## Evidence status

- `designed`: workflow only; no execution.
- `desk-researched`: sources checked; no run.
- `fixture-tested`: runnable against specified local/public fixture.
- `live-tested`: run against named live model/tool/system with version/date.
- `practitioner-reviewed`: reviewed by a relevant practitioner with scope/date.
- `production-validated`: representative production evidence and authorized review.
- `blocked`: required access or authority is unavailable.

Statuses are cumulative only when evidence exists. A fixture-tested course is not live-tested; a live run is not practitioner review.

## Final reviewer questions

- What does a working learner produce in 30-90 minutes?
- What command or action proves it?
- What exact defect makes it fail?
- What does the learner learn that a generic ChatGPT tutorial does not teach?
- Which judgment remains with the professional?
- Which claim would become stale first, and what rerun detects that?
