# TD-F01 · Profession reality before AI transformation

## Research brief

- Controlling question: What does software test development actually own, how is value demonstrated, and which parts are valid AI opportunities?
- Learner: junior-to-mid test engineer or developer moving into quality engineering.
- Professional decision: choose one AI intervention against a real workflow pain without inventing internal process.
- Artifact: profession reality map, internal-information interview list, and measurable AI opportunity backlog.
- Scope boundary: public evidence can reconstruct common work and role progression; it cannot establish the learner's company workflow, permissions, performance system, or promotion rules.

## Source pack

- S32 O*NET occupation profile: current task and work-activity surface including design review, strategy, automation, diagnosis, release readiness, performance, environments and customer issues.
- S90 UK Government Digital and Data capability framework: four public role levels, CI/CD, reusable tools, lifecycle quality, risk methods, standards, communication and progression.
- S91 GitLab historical SET career framework: strategy, coverage, pipeline triage, flaky tests, tooling, environments, OKRs and knowledge sharing. The page is explicitly deprecated because the SET role transitioned to Backend Engineer; used as organization-specific counterevidence, not an industry trend estimate.
- S92 practitioner career-ladder discussion: used only to identify IC-versus-management variation and inconsistent employer ladders.
- S82 ISTQB test analyst syllabus: professional test-analysis baseline and artifact vocabulary.

Excluded as proof: generic AI-for-QA listicles, tool-vendor ROI claims, and forum opinions used as prevalence evidence.

## Evidence synthesis

### Supported facts

The occupation includes much more than test execution. Opened authoritative sources support requirements and design review, risk-informed test design, automation and CI, environment and defect diagnosis, reusable tooling, release-readiness evidence and cross-functional communication. The UK framework supports a progression from supervised contribution to ownership, standards, reusable frameworks, cross-team dependencies and coaching.

### Practitioner and employer signals

GitLab's public historical SET framework exposes concrete work such as pipeline on-call, flaky-test remediation, test tooling, environments and OKRs. Its 2026 role transition is counterevidence to a stable universal “SDET ladder.” The Reddit discussion reinforces that many employers lack a consistent principal QA path, but it is anecdotal.

### Inference

A defensible career curriculum should teach transferable quality leverage: risk decisions, executable evidence, automation/platform thinking, diagnosis, AI evaluation, observability and production feedback. It should not promise a single promotion path or assume a dedicated QA role will remain organizationally separate.

### Unknowns

Target-company workflow, decision rights, release policy, historical incidents, access controls, metrics, performance ratings and promotion evidence are `INTERNAL-UNKNOWN` until internal documents and interviews are supplied.

## Engineering blueprint

The profession-reconstruction pipeline runs before AI scenario ranking:

1. collect public role, capability, employer, community, artifact and course evidence;
2. reconstruct day, sprint/release, deliverable and incident paths;
3. connect triggers, actors, inputs, dependencies, systems, artifacts, decisions and failure costs;
4. separate public progression signals from internal performance rules;
5. classify pains and information barriers;
6. rank AI opportunities by professional leverage, inspectable output, authority boundary and measurable proof;
7. build one beginner starter pack with baseline, failure injection and transfer checklist.

Required stop states: `INTERNAL-UNKNOWN` for hidden organization facts, `BLOCKED` for missing critical inputs or owners, and `NOT_RUN` when no validation has executed.
